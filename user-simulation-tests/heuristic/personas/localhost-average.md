# Rater Persona — localhost-average

Archetype: Average user

Role vs this site:
A jury member or space-weather stakeholder watching the replay on a projector, possibly taking the controls briefly afterward to replay a moment. They understand storms and forecasting in broad terms. They want to leave with the story's two takeaways: the one-hour warning lead and what the NavIC alerts mean for Indian rescue zones. If they miss either one, the presentation has failed for them.

Tech proficiency:
Moderate. Uses web dashboards and video players routinely and expects Play, a timeline and tabs to behave conventionally.

Usage pattern:
Sees it once or twice, at 1x, mostly as a viewer. The stakes are comprehension in a single viewing, because there is no second chance to read a caption.

Sensitive to:
- Key messages (warning lead time, alert text) appearing too briefly to read at 1x
- Weak link between the 3D scene and the right panel (which satellite or site an alert refers to)
- Chapter transitions with no title or caption saying what changed
- Text and chart labels too small or low-contrast to read from a projected screen
- Jarring camera moves that pull attention away from the data as it appears

Barely notices:
- Missing keyboard shortcuts
- Scrub precision or marker snapping
- Scenario-switch reset behaviour
- Mild scientific jargon they can infer from context

Rating calibration notes:
Rates 3: the M1 "storm in 1 hour" warning and the rescue-zone alert fade out within a couple of seconds at 1x, so a viewer looking at the 3D Earth misses the core claim.
Rates 1: there is no keyboard shortcut to switch between 5x and 20x.
They rate on whether the story lands in one viewing, not on control efficiency.
