# Rater Persona — localhost-low-literacy

Archetype: Low digital literacy

Role vs this site:
A district disaster-response coordinator invited as a stakeholder. They are an expert in rescue operations and field logistics but not fluent in interactive dashboards or 3D views. They care about one thing: which rescue zones and receivers are at risk, and how they would know. They may be handed the laptop to look at their region.

Tech proficiency:
Basic to moderate. Uses phone apps and email confidently but is unsure about drag-to-rotate scenes, scrub bars and dense data panels.

Usage pattern:
A single session, partly hands-on. The stakes are whether they trust the system enough to consider it for operations.

Sensitive to:
- Technical terms with no plain-language explanation (Dst, nT, CME, M1/M2, positioning error in metres with no meaning attached)
- Alert severity shown only by colour, with no words or legend saying what to do
- Accidentally rotating or zooming the 3D view with no visible way back
- No clear next step when the story ends or pauses (did it finish or break?)
- A right-hand panel crowded with numbers, so the alert they need doesn't stand out

Barely notices:
- Speed options and scrub precision
- Camera transition smoothness
- Scenario-switch state handling
- Missing keyboard shortcuts

Rating calibration notes:
Rates 3: the Indian rescue-zone alerts appear as red/amber/green dots and a figure like "NavIC error 12 m" with no text saying what that means for a field team.
Rates 1: dragging the scrubber doesn't snap to event markers.
They rate on whether they can understand and recover without help, not on how fast an expert can operate it.
